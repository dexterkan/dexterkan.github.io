01 / FOLLOW THE SIGNAL

Two transmission phases. One RIS configuration.

First, the base station sends data to users and a decode-and-forward relay while the RIS reflects its signal. Next, the relay forwards the decoded data, again assisted by the RIS. The same surface configuration serves both phases.

Why coordinate all three?

RIS phases that strengthen the first transmission can weaken the second. Optimizing the base station or relay alone also leaves interactions and multiuser interference unresolved.

Users combine both received signals using maximal ratio combining: γk = γIk + γIIk (Equation 14).

Alternating optimization (AO)

Hold two variable groups fixed while updating the third, then repeat. Each subproblem uses convex approximations and semidefinite relaxation; randomization recovers beamforming vectors or surface phases.

BS beamforming

Update the base station beamforming while holding the relay and RIS variables fixed.

Algorithm 1. The objective is non-decreasing under the paper’s update rules and bounded, establishing convergence; this does not establish a globally optimal solution.

05 / FORMULATION & SCOPE

Joint optimization with practical surface constraints.

Objective and constraints

Maximize Σk log₂(1 + γk) under separate BS and relay power budgets, discrete unit-modulus RIS phase choices, and a relay decoding SINR threshold. The relay is half-duplex decode-and-forward; the RIS reflects in both phases.

Default settings: BS and relay maximum power 10 mW each; 2-bit RIS phase control; relay decoding threshold 0.01; noise power 2 × 10⁻⁵. RIS-related channels use quasi-static Rician fading; other channels use Rayleigh fading.

Energy efficiency and evaluation scope

The EE extension uses a Dinkelbach transform and an AO procedure to optimize utility relative to BS, relay, and RIS power consumption. RIS element power is 0.05 mW in the EE experiment.

Results are numerical simulations, rather than a deployed radio testbed. The main formulation assumes known CSI; Figure 11 examines imperfect RIS–user CSI. Semidefinite relaxation, randomization, and discrete-phase projection introduce approximation into a non-convex design problem.
